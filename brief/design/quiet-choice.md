# Quiet Choice

A choice is a mark, not a container. Quiet Choice removes the boxes that fence each option and lets a single ring carry the whole act of choosing: open while the question is open, filled once the reader commits. The page keeps one frame, the card, and inside it nothing competes with the question. Options read as lines of a sentence the reader is about to finish. The work must look meticulously crafted, as if every hairline and every pixel of air had been argued over by someone at the top of the craft.

Space does the separating. Options sit on a steady vertical rhythm with generous leading, aligned to one invisible column so the eye runs straight down the rings. Hover is a breath of tint, not an outline; the row warms slightly and nothing shifts. The ring is drawn with a fine, even stroke at a size that matches the text's x-height, centred on the first line and never on the paragraph, so long answers hang cleanly beside it. This alignment is painstaking work: the product of deep expertise, measured, not guessed.

Colour arrives only with meaning. Before the reader chooses, everything is ink on paper: one neutral for the ring, one for the text, a fainter one for the small letter that names each option. After the choice, two colours speak and only where they must: the ring of the right answer fills with green, the reader's wrong pick fills with red, and every other option steps back into a softer grey. No badges, no banners; the state lives in the ring and in the dimming around it, executed with master-level restraint.

Type stays calm and singular. The question is set a step heavier; the options keep the reading face at the reading size; the option letters shrink to a small monospace whisper beside the ring, present for reference and never for decoration. Text is sparse and exact, and every word that remains carries weight. Nothing is bolded to look important.

The result should feel like a printed form refined over many editions: familiar enough that no one needs to be taught it, precise enough that a designer notices the care. Light and dark are one design, recalibrated rather than inverted, so the ring, the tint and the two signal colours hold the same weight on either ground. Every state, untouched, hovered, chosen, revealed, must look as considered as the first, the evidence of painstaking attention to the smallest mark.
